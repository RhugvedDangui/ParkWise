1. Visual Design & Overall Feel
The aesthetic should be modern, clean, and highly functional—similar to leading navigation apps like Google Maps or Waze, but hyper-focused on parking.

Color Palette: Use a dark mode by default (or auto-switch based on time of day) to reduce glare while driving at night.

Color-Coded Status: Rely heavily on universal color psychology:

Green: High availability / Free spots.

Orange/Yellow: Filling up fast / Limited spots.

Red: Full / No parking.

Blue: EV Charging stations.

Typography: Large, highly legible, sans-serif fonts. Drivers need to read information at a quick glance.

Touch Targets: Buttons and interactive elements must be large and easy to tap with one hand.

2. Key Screens & UI Components
A. The Home Screen (The Map View)
This is the core of the app. The map should take up 80–90% of the screen real estate.

Interactive Map: A smooth 2D/3D map showing streets, buildings, and parking zones.

Smart Search Bar (Top): "Where do you want to park?" with quick-select icons for "Home," "Work," or "Saved Locations."

Quick Filters (Below Search): Scrollable pill-shaped buttons for instant filtering (e.g., EV Only, Covered Parking, Free Spots, Under $5/hr).

Dynamic Map Markers: Pins showing real-time spot availability. Tapping a pin should slightly zoom in and bring up the details.

Heatmap Overlay Toggle: A floating button on the map to switch on the "Congestion Heatmap," shading streets in red, yellow, or green based on traffic/parking density.

Floating Action Buttons (FABs):

Recenter Location button.

Report Spot button (large, easily accessible).

B. Spot Details & Action Panel (Bottom Sheet)
When a user taps a parking pin, a card should slide up from the bottom of the screen.

Basic Info: Name/Address of the lot or street.

Predictive Gauge: A visual meter saying "Spot likely to fill in 10 mins" based on your predictive AI.

Metrics: Price per hour, distance from destination, and estimated walking time to the final destination.

Live Updates: "Last updated 2 mins ago by user / sensor."

Call-to-Action (CTA) Buttons: Two massive, primary buttons: "Navigate Here" and "Reserve Spot" (if applicable).

C. The Crowdsourcing / Reporting UI
This needs to be extremely frictionless so users actually use it.

1-Tap Actions: A quick pop-up asking, "Leaving your spot?" with "Yes" and "No" buttons.

Gamification Banner: A small UI element thanking the user and showing points earned (e.g., "+10 ParkWise Points for updating!").

D. Driving & Navigation Mode
Once the user hits "Navigate," the UI must shift into a minimal driving mode.

Turn-by-Turn Directions: Clear, bold arrows and street names.

Auto-Rerouting Alert: A pop-up notification at the top: "Target spot just filled. Rerouting to nearest available spot (2 mins away)."

Arrival Screen: Upon arriving, the app should automatically prompt the payment/timer screen or ask the user to confirm they parked successfully.

E. User Profile & Wallet
A slide-out menu or separate tab for user management.

Digital Wallet: Current balance, linked credit cards, and parking receipt history.

Vehicle Profiles: Let users save their car dimensions (compact vs. SUV) or EV plug type, so the app only shows spots their car can fit in.
